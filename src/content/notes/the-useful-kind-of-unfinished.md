There is an unfinished project that waits in a folder because nobody, including its maker, can tell what it does. There is another kind you can use on a Tuesday even though its edges are still being worked out.

I want more of the second kind on my desk.

## Give it a working middle

For [the pocket field guide](/projects/pocket-field-guide/), the middle is simple: take a walk, choose one thing, write down what you can actually observe. A page that supports that sequence is useful before the cover, binding, or indexing system has been settled.

This helps me choose what to do next. If a change makes the walk easier, it belongs near the front of the queue. If it mostly makes the project look finished in a photograph, it can wait.

The same test works for a reading list. Can I find the book? Can I remember why I added it? If those two questions have answers, the list has begun doing its job.

## Say where the edge is

An honest project note names what exists and what is still an idea. It shouldn't make a sketch sound like a download, or an intention sound like a release. I like keeping a little “next attempt” section beside the thing itself. That gives a visitor context and gives me somewhere to resume.

There's a difference between leaving a loose end and leaving somebody confused. A clear description can make an early project inviting without pretending it is complete.

## Stop at a useful place

My stopping point is a small version I could explain to a friend without apologizing for every choice. Then I write down the next question and put it away.

If the question keeps returning, I'll work on it again. If it doesn't, the useful part can stay. [Keeping a homepage](/notes/a-homepage-is-a-habit/) gives these modest things a home without turning every hobby into an obligation.
