This project is a way of keeping reading notes without making a scoreboard. I want to remember why a book entered my life, what I noticed while reading, and whether I want to come back.

The list starts on paper. The entries worth sharing can move into [the collection](/collections/).

## Keep the reason

Each entry gets a title, an author, and one sentence beginning with “I'm curious about…” That sentence is more useful to me than a rating. It reminds me what I hoped to find when a book has waited on a shelf for a while.

During reading, I add page references and questions. I avoid copying long passages into the list; a short description helps me find the section again without pretending my note can replace it. If a thought becomes a separate piece of writing, I link the two.

My entry for [Braiding Sweetgrass](/collections/braiding-sweetgrass/) is an example of the kind of attention I mean: reading an essay, putting the book down, and letting a question travel into the rest of the day.

## Use gentle statuses

The only statuses I need are “might read,” “reading,” “put aside,” and “return to.” Finishing is something I can record in a sentence. It doesn't have to be the organizing principle.

“Put aside” is especially useful. It leaves room for the wrong season, an interrupted week, or a book I simply don't want to finish. None of those require a verdict on the book itself.

## Where it goes next

I'd like to make a single printable page with enough room for several titles and their reasons. Before that, I'm testing which fields I actually use. A large form would be easy to design and annoying to keep.

For finding titles, I've kept a few starting points in [the link garden](/garden/). For finding unexpected neighbors, I'll take an afternoon among [library shelves](/collections/public-libraries/).
