This is a working set of prompts for an ordinary notebook. The aim is to make a walk a little more attentive without packing a bag full of equipment or turning it into homework.

The current version is the page structure below. A printed booklet is still an idea.

## The page

Begin with a date and a broad place description. Then make room for four things: what you noticed, a small drawing, something you're unsure about, and a question to bring home.

Keep the observation separate from the guess. “Three narrow leaves, pale underneath” will still be useful if the name you first imagined turns out to be wrong. A drawing can show a shape you don't have words for yet. It doesn't have to look good.

I use [a pocket notebook](/collections/a-pocket-notebook/) because a page I can reach easily is more likely to get used. A folded sheet works too. The format should fit the walk, rather than dictate it.

## Try it close to home

Choose one thing you can see from a path or a public place. Spend a few minutes looking before reaching for a search. Note the weather only if it helps describe what you saw. Leave plants, objects, and places as you found them.

At home, look at your question again. Does it need a name, a comparison, or another visit? Add whatever you learn beneath the original observation so you can see how your understanding changed.

## The next attempt

I'd like to test a page with more drawing room and fewer printed prompts. The open question is whether a little structure helps after the first few walks, or starts getting in the way.

[Taking the long way home](/notes/taking-the-long-way-home/) explains the habit behind this project. For now, the most useful result would be a notebook with a few uneven drawings and questions worth returning to.
